\section{Datasets}

Experiments were conducted using the vanilla Minecraft 1.21 jar as the base (content) pack, but the evaluation corpus spans multiple target packs rather than a single Ashen-only case study. Two checkpoint families are relevant in this report. \texttt{run20} is the diffusion-only sweep, which emitted validation previews for seven target packs. \texttt{run33} is the later dual-model configuration, which completed 10,000-step diffusion validation for four packs and 2,000-step recolor-only validation for Clarity. The target packs cover minimalist, vanilla-adjacent, cartoon, fantasy, and higher-resolution stylized packs, so the report can describe both general trends and pack-specific failure modes.

\section{Training Configuration}

Both models were trained on a single NVIDIA GPU with bfloat16 autocast. The diffusion model (StyleAwareUNet, 7.7M parameters) was trained for 10,000 steps with gradient accumulation of 4 (effective batch size 16), while RecolorNet (1.0M parameters) was trained for 2,500--5,000 steps with batch size 8. \section{Evolution of Training Runs}

\subsection{Diffusion Baseline}

The initial wide sweep used a diffusion-only model with a linear noise schedule and base channel count 128. No post-hoc selector or recolor fallback was available. The main value of \texttt{run20} is not a single headline number, but the fact that the same model family was exposed to seven distinct pack styles. Minimalist and vanilla-adjacent targets converged quickly: Bare Bones and Faithful already looked coherent at their latest preview steps, while stylized packs with stronger palette shifts such as Ashen, Cartoon, and Chibli showed more visible drift, especially on noisy materials and fine outlines. This broad run established that \emph{pack difficulty is not uniform}; the training recipe that is comfortable for Bare Bones is not automatically adequate for a 64$\times$ stylized pack compressed to $32\times32$.

\subsection{Dual-Model Architecture}

The next iteration introduced RecolorNet as a structural fallback together with the content-aware loss suite (structure, contrast, hue, color moment). This reduced hue instability and helped the diffusion branch preserve stronger edge structure, but it also made clear that a fixed routing threshold was too crude across multiple packs. Hard textures such as \texttt{bookshelf.png} and \texttt{tnt\_side.png} benefit from deterministic structure preservation, whereas easy textures in minimalist packs can be handled by diffusion alone. A pack-blind selector therefore risks being either too permissive on difficult packs or too conservative on easy ones.

\subsection{Post-Hoc Quality Evaluation}

The next iteration replaced the fixed routing logic with the structural-style evaluator described in Section~\ref{sec:inference}. This evaluator scores the actual diffusion output against the content image and its support set, then decides whether to keep diffusion, apply detail injection, or fall back to recolor. In practice, this was the first configuration that made multi-pack comparison intellectually honest: the same selection policy can now be inspected on Bare Bones, Ashen, Cartoon, and Chibli rather than only on a hand-picked pack. Training improvements included EMA (decay 0.995), linear learning-rate warmup over 5\% of steps, and an adaptive content-loss ramp over the early phase of training.

\subsection{High-Pattern Content Gating}

The post-hoc evaluator was further refined to detect and tightly screen \emph{high-pattern content}---textures with strong line art, symbolic glyphs, or repeated narrow bands such as \texttt{tnt\_side.png} and \texttt{bookshelf.png}. When the content texture exceeds thresholds on edge energy, detail energy, and complexity, the evaluator applies a multiplicative gate (0.55 + 0.45 $\times$ gate\_score) to the quality score and raises the acceptance threshold to 0.68. The gate score combines tighter edge ratio fidelity (window $[0.78, 1.18]$), detail energy ratio fidelity (window $[0.80, 1.16]$), and a positive detail coherence gate. This prevents the selector from accepting diffusion outputs that pass the general quality threshold but are structurally unsuitable for precision-dependent textures. The source-relative change agreement score was also added at this stage, blending the support descriptor comparison with a term that rewards candidates whose change-from-vanilla magnitude matches the support pack's typical change level.

\subsection{SNR-Weighted Losses and Final Configuration}

The final configuration used in \texttt{run33} introduced SNR-weighted $x_0$ auxiliary losses, meaning reconstruction and content losses are scaled by $\bar{\alpha}_t$, the signal-to-noise ratio at the current diffusion timestep. This prevents the model from over-committing to unreliable $x_0$ estimates at high noise levels. The content loss weight was reduced from 0.40 to 0.20 and the content-loss components were rebalanced to weights 1.0 / 2.5 / 3.0 / 1.5 for structure / contrast / hue / color-moment.

Additional refinements included:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Symmetric detail energy fidelity in the post-hoc evaluator (penalizing both blur and noise, rather than only blur).
    \item Combined candidate selection using the sum of structural and style quality scores.
    \item A softer contrast gate with $\gamma=1.5$ (reduced from 2.0) and gate floor 0.05.
    \item Increased detail emphasis scale to 2.0.
\end{itemize}

Bare Bones is by far the easiest target family: it already performs best in the diffusion-only run, and it remains the cleanest pack in \texttt{run33}. \section{Qualitative Analysis}

\subsection{What This Model is Suited For}

\textbf{Vanilla-adjacent and minimalist packs.} Bare Bones, Faithful, and parts of Lithos are structurally close enough to vanilla that the system can focus on palette and contrast remapping rather than inventing new geometry. This is why Bare Bones dominates both \texttt{run20} and \texttt{run33}, and why Faithful already looks solid after only a 500-step diffusion snapshot in \texttt{run20}.

\textbf{Medium-complexity stylized materials.} Ashen and Cartoon benefit from the added content-aware losses because they require both strong palette movement and controlled edge placement. Wood, stone, and ore textures usually preserve the correct large-scale structure while moving into the correct pack-specific value range.

\textbf{Deterministic or blend-assisted routing.} The later selector is effective when diffusion color is useful but raw diffusion structure is risky. The saved \texttt{run33} outputs for Bare Bones show that this often manifests as blended candidates rather than a hard switch to pure recolor, which is the desired regime for easy packs.

\subsection{What This Model Struggles With}

\textbf{High-resolution packs compressed to $32\times32$.} Chibli is the clearest example. The pack has only 87 training pairs after preprocessing and starts from 64$\times$ artwork. Even when the diffusion candidate has respectable overall style, the downscaled microtexture and line work remain difficult to preserve.

\textbf{Transparent or pseudo-transparent materials.} Glass remains a fundamental problem because alpha is discarded before modeling. The network must hallucinate a fully opaque RGB realization of a texture whose appearance in the target pack is often driven more by transparency treatment than by raw color.

\textbf{Fine symbolic detail.} \texttt{tnt\_side.png} and \texttt{bookshelf.png} remain good stress tests because they require preserving legible letters or narrow repeated book spines. In those cases, the selector usually prefers recolor or blend candidates over raw diffusion.

\section{Ablation Studies}

\subsection{Selection Policy Versus Oracle Error}

The cross-pack comparison reveals a useful tension: the selector is not optimizing target-aware MAE directly. Bare Bones and Chibli both show lower average MAE for the raw diffusion branch than for the selected output, but the selected outputs are structurally safer and more regular. This is not necessarily a bug; it reflects the design goal of preferring plausible, tileable textures over target-aware oracle error. It does mean, however, that future work should calibrate the selector separately for easy packs and hard packs rather than using one conservative policy everywhere.

\subsection{Detail Injection Usage}

The saved selected panels show that detail injection is not a rare corner case. In \texttt{run33}, 16 of 18 Bare Bones selections and 6 of 19 Cartoon selections differ from both the raw diffusion and raw recolor panels, indicating blended candidates. On Ashen and Chibli, by contrast, the selector usually settles on the pure recolor output. This suggests the blend path is most valuable when the pack remains close enough to vanilla for diffusion detail to be trusted locally.

The non-destructive detail injection (Section~\ref{sec:inference}) was critical for this result. Earlier variants that rebuilt the output from a blurred diffusion base caused global softening on already-good diffusion samples. The current approach adds only missing high-frequency residuals where local layout agreement and detail alignment gates permit, preserving diffusion detail that is already plausible.

\subsection{Pack-Dependent Loss Interaction}

The multi-pack comparison supports the concern raised during development that extra structural penalties can backfire on very easy packs. Bare Bones is already strong in \texttt{run20}; the later configuration improves robustness but does not produce a dramatic numeric gain. Ashen is the opposite case: it benefits clearly from the added structure and hue guidance. This is strong evidence that future training should scale or schedule content losses based on pack complexity rather than applying one fixed recipe to all packs.

\section{Limitations of Current Approach}

\textbf{No alpha-channel modeling.} Despite preprocessing and storing alpha channels in the dataset, the model operates on RGB only. Transparent textures (glass, ice, nether portal) are inherently ill-posed in RGB space.

\textbf{Heuristic support ranking.} The descriptor-based ranking with filename priors is effective but brittle. Packs using nonstandard naming conventions or novel material families may receive suboptimal support assignments. A learned ranking model could improve generalization.

\textbf{Pack-specific training.} Each target pack requires a separate training run. While this enables pack-specific specialization (which is the project's stated goal), it precludes zero-shot generalization to unseen packs.

\textbf{Resolution ceiling.} The fixed $32\times32$ resolution limits detail fidelity for packs with complex microtexture (leaves, bookshelves). A multi-resolution training scheme could address this but would increase computational cost proportionally.

\textbf{Evaluation artifacts are preview-first.} Several runs store validation as composite preview panels rather than as standalone metric JSON files for every pack and every candidate. That is adequate for human inspection and for the post-hoc analysis performed here, but a future report-oriented pipeline should export raw per-pack metrics directly to avoid reconstruction work.
